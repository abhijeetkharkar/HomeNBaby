## 3. Soil Mix for 5 Raised Beds (No Hugelkultur)

### Bed specs — FINAL
- 5 beds × 8 ft × 3 ft
- **All beds 12" deep** (Bed 2 must be 12" for carrots/mooli; all others matched for consistency)
- Total volume: 5 × 8 × 3 × 1 ft = **120 cu ft = 4.44 cu yards**

---

### Soil mix — NO vermiculite

Vermiculite is expensive and hard to source in bulk in Texas. For the crop mix (tomatoes, brinjal, gourds, cucumbers, okra) in Texas heat, a **60% compost + 40% topsoil** mix works better anyway — vermiculite retains too much moisture in summer and can cause root rot.

| Bed | Crops | Mix |
|-----|-------|-----|
| Bed 1 | Tomatoes | 60% compost + 40% topsoil |
| **Bed 2** | **Carrots + Mooli** | **50% compost + 50% coarse river sand** (no topsoil — needs loose, deep, stone-free medium) |
| Bed 3 | Cucumbers | 60% compost + 40% topsoil |
| Bed 4 | Gourds (Karela, Dudhi, Dodka) | 60% compost + 40% topsoil |
| Bed 5 | Gourds (Ghosale) + Okra | 60% compost + 40% topsoil |

## 6. Scenario C — Using Native Soil as Bed Base (if grass is NOT kikuyu / nut grass / bermuda)

If the yard has a benign grass (St. Augustine, fescue, ryegrass, or bare soil patches), you can incorporate the native soil into the bed instead of buying full volume — significantly reducing cost.

### How to identify if it's safe to use
- **Safe to use:** St. Augustine (broad flat blades), tall fescue, annual ryegrass, bare compacted clay/loam
- **NOT safe (remove completely):** Bermuda (wiry, spreads by runners + rhizomes), nut grass/nutsedge (triangular stem, impossible to kill), kikuyu (aggressive stolons)
- If unsure — pull a clump and look for **white underground runners/rhizomes**. If present, treat it as unsafe.

---

### Grass removal method (safe grass, no herbicide)

**Best method: Sod cutter + sheet mulch — NOT a tiller**

1. **Mark the bed outline** with stakes + string
2. **Scalp the grass** with a flat spade or sod cutter — slice horizontally 1–2" below the surface, remove sod in strips. Shake off soil from roots, compost the sod or discard.
3. **Do NOT rototill** — tilling chops grass roots into hundreds of pieces, each of which can re-sprout. This is how you turn a manageable grass problem into an unmanageable one.
4. **Sheet mulch the exposed soil** — lay 4–6 sheets of plain cardboard (no tape/staples) directly on the bare ground. Overlap edges by 6". This smothers any missed roots and feeds soil biology as it breaks down.
5. **Place bed frame on top of cardboard** — the cardboard becomes the bottom layer, no separate weed barrier needed for the first year.
6. **Fill bed on top** — your compost + topsoil mix goes directly over the cardboard.

---

### How deep to dig (and whether it matters)

| Bed depth | Do you need to dig at all? |
|-----------|---------------------------|
| **12" raised bed** | **No digging needed** — 12" of growing mix above cardboard is sufficient for all your crops except Bed 2 |
| **Bed 2 (carrots + mooli)** | Yes — after removing sod, **fork-loosen native soil to 12"** below the bed base. Carrots/mooli roots go 10–14" deep and will hit compacted clay if you don't loosen it. No need to remove the native soil, just break it up. |
| **8" raised bed** | Light loosening recommended — roots of tomatoes and gourds will hit the native soil layer and appreciate it being broken up |

> For a 12" bed, roots of tomatoes, gourds, brinjal won't even reach the native soil layer during the main growing season — you don't need to dig at all except Bed 2.

---

### Does buying a tiller make sense?

**No — not for this use case.** Here's why:

| | Tiller | Flat spade / sod cutter |
|--|--------|------------------------|
| Grass removal | ❌ Chops roots, causes regrowth | ✅ Removes cleanly |
| Soil loosening under Bed 2 | Overkill for one bed | ✅ Garden fork does it in 20 min |
| Cost | $200–400 to buy / $60–80 to rent | You likely have a spade |
| Ongoing use | No other use case in a raised bed garden | — |

**Rent a sod cutter** from Home Depot (~$80/day) if you have all 5 bed footprints to clear — it's faster and cleaner than a spade for large areas. For 5 beds × 8×3 ft = 120 sq ft total, an hour with a rented sod cutter handles it. No need to own anything.

---

### Soil volume reduction if using native soil base

If you remove sod and loosen native soil but don't dig out, the native soil contributes ~3–4" to the root zone effectively. You can **reduce your imported mix by ~25%**:

| Material | Full import (no native) | With native soil base |
|----------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Aged compost | 2.75 cu yd | **2.0 cu yd** |
| Topsoil / sandy loam | 1.5 cu yd | **0.75 cu yd** |
| Coarse river sand (Bed 2) | 0.5 cu yd | 0.5 cu yd (unchanged) |
| **Total** | **4.75 cu yd (~$277)** | **~3.25 cu yd (~$195)** |

> **Savings: ~$80 on materials** if native soil is safe to use.
